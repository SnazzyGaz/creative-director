# Film notes (optional depth): look vocabulary and myths

Reference lists for `modules/film/`, moved out of `edit.md` in v0.9.7 so the rules load lighter. Load when a brief asks for "a film look", or before calling something a film rule. Sources as in the module README.

## Look vocabulary (by technique, not by title)

When a brief asks for "a film look", name the technique, not a title: high-key studio light; low-key chiaroscuro; available-light naturalism; day-for-night; bleach-bypass (silver retained: low saturation, high contrast); saturated dye-transfer primaries; cross-processed colour shifts; push-processed grain; black-and-white with filters; halation and bloom around highlights; diffusion filtering; anamorphic flares and oval bokeh. The `lookbook` module has no film entries yet; adding them would need example plates, which this version does not include.

## Myths and weak claims

Don't use these as reasons; the same stills myths apply too (`core/evidence.md`).

- **The rule of thirds is the law of framing.** It is a rough guide for some shots. Eyelines, looking room and the matching reverse shot decide placement (FILM-SHOT-6, FILM-COMP-4). See `core/evidence.md`.
- **The golden ratio or spiral is how great shots are framed.** Overlays fitted after the fact prove nothing; preference studies don't support it [SRC-220, SRC-38].
- **24 fps is sacred.** It became the standard in the sound era as a compromise of cost and sound quality [SRC-312]; viewers preferred higher rates in stereoscopic tests [SRC-311]. It is a strong convention with a familiar feel, not a property of "cinema".
- **Shallow depth of field makes it cinematic.** It is one tool. Deep staging is as old and as cinematic, and shallow focus on every shot flattens the beats (FILM-SHOT-4).
- **Teal and orange always looks good.** It separates warm skin from cool shadows, but applied by default it is a cliché and fights many stories [SRC-310]. The palette comes from the story (FILM-STORY-3).
- **"Film look" means grain, letterbox bars and a LUT.** The look comes from light, lens, staging, movement and grade working together; bars on a 16:9 frame just crop it.
- **The Kuleshov effect proves editing can make any face mean anything.** The effect is real but modest and uneven, and the original experiment can't be checked [SRC-293 to SRC-296].
- **Wide lenses distort and long lenses compress.** Camera distance does; the lens only crops (FILM-SHOT-3) [SRC-313].
- **Colours carry fixed meanings (red is danger, blue is sad).** Shared associations exist across cultures but vary with language and place, and context and the film's own pairings decide meaning [SRC-304, SRC-46].
- **Never cross the line.** It is a convention for readable space, broken well when the beat wants it (FILM-CON-1).
- **Faster cutting is more exciting.** Corpus studies show pace has risen, not that faster is better; shots cut before they are read lose the viewer (FILM-EDIT-3) [SRC-284, SRC-286].
- **Murch's percentages are measured.** They are a deliberately playful ranking (FILM-EDIT-1) [SRC-269].
- **Handheld means realism; low angle means power.** Each can, depending on context and use [SRC-290, SRC-291, SRC-325].
