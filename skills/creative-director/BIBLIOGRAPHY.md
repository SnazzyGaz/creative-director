# Bibliography: composition, colour and beauty

An annotated reading list behind `modules/composition/`, `modules/colour-value/` and the v0.5.0 rules in the other modules. Each entry has its source ID (full citation in `SOURCES.md`), where to read it, and one line on what it is good for.

**How to use it.** The rules paraphrase these works and link to them; short quotations come only from public-domain texts. Old books are wise but untested: the modules grade each claim (well supported, partly supported, craft wisdom, contested / myth; legend in `core/evidence.md`). Where an old book and an experiment disagree, the experiment wins for claims about viewers, and the book still wins as a source of methods to try.

**Rights notes.** Works published before 1931 are public domain in the United States. Elsewhere, the term is usually the author's life plus 70 years; check local law before reusing images or long passages. Links go to Project Gutenberg (gutenberg.org) or the Internet Archive (archive.org).

## 1. Composition, balance and beauty: the public-domain classics

| ID | Work | Read it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| S181 | Arthur Wesley Dow, *Composition* (1899; 1913 ed.) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/45410) · [Archive](https://archive.org/details/compositionserie00dowauoft) | The American bridge to Japanese design: line, notan (dark-light) and colour, with five principles built on good spacing. The source of COMP-6, COMP-8, COMP-10 and CV-2. |
| S182 | Denman W. Ross, *A Theory of Pure Design* (1907) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/74765) | Harmony, balance and rhythm defined with diagrams; the "centre of equilibrium". COMP-2, COMP-11. |
| S183 | Henry R. Poore, *Pictorial Composition and the Critical Judgment of Pictures* (1903) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/26638) | The steelyard and the quadrant-line test for balance, and the studio viewing tests (mirror, upside down, over the shoulder). COMP-2, ILL-15. |
| S184 | John Ruskin, *The Elements of Drawing* (1857) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/30325) | Letter III's nine "laws of composition", principality first. COMP-4, COMP-8, COMP-10. |
| S185 | William Hogarth, *The Analysis of Beauty* (1753) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/51459) | Six principles that correct each other, and the serpentine line; the first modern attempt at a theory of visual beauty. COMP-9. |
| S186 | Francis Hutcheson, *An Inquiry into the Original of Our Ideas of Beauty and Virtue* (1725) | [Archive](https://archive.org/details/inquiryintoorigi1726hutc) | "Uniformity amidst variety", the oldest version of the order-plus-complexity idea now studied in the lab. COMP-9. |
| S187 | Edmund Burke, *A Philosophical Enquiry into ... the Sublime and Beautiful* (1757) | [Archive](https://archive.org/details/enqphilosophical00burkrich) | Separates the beautiful (smooth, gradual, small) from the sublime (vast, dark, overwhelming): two aims a brief can ask for. |
| S196 | Walter Crane, *Line and Form* (1900); *The Bases of Design* (1898) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25290) · [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/47967) | Line as the backbone of design; rhythm and space-filling. COMP-11. |
| S197 | Ernest A. Batchelder, *Design in Theory and Practice* (1910) | [Archive](https://archive.org/details/designintheorypr00batcuoft) | A clear Arts and Crafts course in rhythm, balance and tone measures. |
| S207 | Roger Fry, *Vision and Design* (1920) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54154) | "An Essay in Aesthetics": line, mass, space, light and colour as the emotional elements of design. |
| S202 | Vitruvius, *The Ten Books on Architecture* (trans. Morgan, 1914) | [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/20239) | Symmetria (parts that share a module) and eurythmy. The honest origin of "proportion", with no golden ratio in it. COMP-16. |
| S194 | Jay Hambidge, *Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase* (1920); *Practical Applications* (1932) | [Archive](https://archive.org/details/cu31924019526882) · [Archive](https://archive.org/details/practicalapplica00hamb) | A usable system of root rectangles; its history of Greek design is not established. Read as a method, not as evidence. COMP-16. |

## 6. Empirical aesthetics: where to start

- **Overviews:** Palmer, Schloss & Sammartino 2013 [S217]; Leder et al. 2004 and Leder & Nadal 2014 [S258]; Wagemans et al. 2012 on Gestalt [S250].
- **Golden ratio:** Green 1995 [S220]; Höge 1997 [S221]; Markowsky 1992 [S222]; Falbo 2005 [S223]; Fischler 1981 [S224]; McManus et al. 2010 [S38].
- **Balance:** McManus et al. 1985 [S235]; Locher et al. 1996 and 1998 [S236, S263]; Wilson & Chatterjee 2005 and Hübner & Fillinger 2016 [S237]; Gershoni & Hochstein 2011 [S238].
- **Optical correction:** Material Design icon keylines [S265].
- **Framing and direction:** Palmer et al. 2008 [S218]; Sammartino & Palmer 2012 [S219]; Chokron & De Agostini 2000 [S254].
- **Attention and gaze:** Cerf et al. 2009 [S239]; Friesen & Kingstone 1998 [S240]; DeAngelus & Pelz 2009 [S241]; Massaro et al. 2012 [S243].
- **Complexity, order, novelty:** Nadal et al. 2010 [S230]; Van Geert & Wagemans 2020 [S231]; Hekkert et al. 2003 [S232]; Reber et al. 2004 [S37].
- **Curvature, symmetry, fractals:** Chuquichambi et al. 2022 [S225]; Jacobsen & Höfel 2002 [S234]; Spehar et al. 2003 and 2016 [S228].
- **Colour:** Schloss & Palmer [S41]; Ou & Luo 2006 [S245]; Birch 2012 [S252].
- **Whose taste:** Vessel et al. 2018 [S251].

Gombrich, *The Sense of Order* (1979), Matila Ghyka, *The Geometry of Art and Life* (1946):** in copyright and not consulted directly for this release. Ghyka's golden-ratio claims are covered by the reviews in section 6.
