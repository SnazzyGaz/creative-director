# Energy pass: eye-catch after the checks pass

**Purpose:** the loop's checks make work accurate, clear and uncluttered. On their own they can stop at "correct and plain". In the repo's blind test (`examples/blind-test/`) the owner of the work often called the with-skill versions sterile, clinical or academic, with basic type and a clip-art feel, and the test ended 10 to 10; where the version without the skill won, the owner's notes mostly named its eye-catch, energy or obvious call to action. This pass runs once, after accuracy and clarity pass, and pushes the piece back toward energy without letting clutter, invented content or the generated look back in.

Format: **What / Check / Fix / Why / Grade**. EN-1 to EN-3 are Procedures (`evidence.md`); the Why lines say how far the cited studies go.

### EN-1. Run it after the checks, where it applies
- **What:** Run it once the blockers and the brief check pass (MUST-BE-RIGHT, CD-1, FP-3, AES-10), before the Sitting right pass and delivery. It is part of the loop, not a new round: its fixes count toward the round's three changes.
  - **Full pass:** posters, flyers, covers, packaging, web heroes and landing pages, social cards, picture books, character art, editorial illustration, public-facing infographics.
  - **Light pass** (EN-2 items 1, 2, 3 and 6 only): official notices, dashboards, data infographics.
  - **Skip:** forms, dense tables, legal or medical text, settings screens, accessibility-critical UI, and any piece where calm is the job. "Quieter" from the owner wins (CMD-2).
- **Check:** Is the scope (full, light, skip) written in the critique with its reason?
- **Why:** A quiet brief still needs a clear focus and chosen type; quiet is not sterile. But energy pushed onto a form or a data table costs the task (UX-1, CORE-8).

### EN-2. The checklist (yes or no on the render)
1. **Focal hook:** one element (image, word or number) leads decisively at 150-200 px wide and in the blur test, not only slightly (CORE-3, AES-2).
2. **Confident display type:** the headline is set in a display face or weight chosen for the brief, a clear step above the next level (poster scale, TYPE-2), not body type enlarged or a default sans set plainly (TYPE-9).
3. **Purposeful colour:** one accent pushed at the focus (CV-6), or colour that codes meaning (bins, categories, routes) backed by a second cue (CV-9). Not timid greys or a palette dulled to be safe (AES-3).
4. **Movement:** at least one of a diagonal, an overlap, a bold crop off the edge, a large scale contrast or a figure in action, and it serves the idea (COMP-12, COMP-20).
5. **Call to action:** if the piece asks for something (book, come, buy, scan, report), what to do and how is found within a few seconds, second only to the hook.
6. **Identity:** an official or branded piece shows who it is from where a viewer looks for it: the issuer's name, or its mark from the official file (BRAND-4). Never an invented mark (BRAND-8).
7. **Mood and role:** the warmth matches the brief: welcoming means warm light and an inhabited place, not empty or derelict. A character's role reads from one or two clear cues, not a pile of props. For young children: few, big, simple shapes.
- **Fix:** One or two changes at a time (AES-10), by restyling what the brief gives: scale, crop, angle, weight, colour, light. Compare old and new side by side for the brief (AES-9).
- **Why:** In eye-tracking studies of print ads, the picture captured attention whatever its size, and attention to text grew with its surface size [S333]. A designed, structured image raised attention and liking, while dense pixel-level detail lowered them [S332]. These are ads seen in magazines, one study each: a direction, not a law.

### EN-3. Guardrails: energy without slop
- **What:** Energy comes from structure (shapes, scale, arrangement, one strong colour), not from surface detail (texture, noise, glow, many hues, extra objects) [S332]. After the pass:
  - still one idea, and nothing added only to be loud (CORE-8, CMD-1 check 3);
  - no added story, text, badges or claims (AES-10);
  - no over-render: grain, speckle, noisy micro-detail, airbrushed gloss, glow on every edge, sparkles, bokeh (ILL-11);
  - no generic generated layout: a centred hero in a radial burst, every surface textured, floating particles, ribbons and seals nobody asked for, fake small print (UI-7, TYPE-9).
- **Check:** The auto check has no new FAIL; the five tests still pass; the piece reads in the thumbnail at least as well as before.
- **The failure pair.** Both ends fail; aim for the middle column.

| Sterile (over-corrected) | Aim | Slop (under-corrected) |
|---|---|---|
| Plain sans at body weight, a timid headline | One display face, a big step in size | Many effects, outlines, gradients on type |
| Greys or one dull hue | One accent at the focus, or a colour code | Every hue at full saturation |
| Static, centred, all elements one size | One hero, the rest clearly smaller | Everything big and competing |
| Empty flat ground, clip-art icons | Simple shapes in one drawn style | Grain, speckle, glossy rendered icons |
| The action buried in small text | The action second in the hierarchy | The action shouting among fake badges |

- **Grade:** Procedure. Internal evidence: owner judgements on 20 briefs, one sample each per arm, 10 to 10, no clear result (`examples/blind-test/`); the sterile-versus-slop pattern comes from the owner's notes, not from a measured effect. Ad studies [S332, S333]: partly supported (one large study each, ads only). Related: "most advanced, yet acceptable" (AES-11, [S232]); lower perceived complexity helps first impressions [S35].
