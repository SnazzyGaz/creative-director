# Energy pass: eye-catch after the checks pass

**Purpose:** the loop's checks make work accurate, clear and uncluttered. On their own they can stop at "correct and plain". In the repo's blind test (`examples/blind-test/`) the owner of the work often called the with-skill versions sterile, clinical or academic, with basic type and a clip-art feel, and batches 1 and 2 ended 10 to 10; where the version without the skill won, the owner's notes mostly named its eye-catch, energy or obvious call to action. With this pass added, batch 3 went 8 to 1 for the skill, with one tie (mostly slight picks; see the grades below). The owner's batch 3 notes then named new gaps, added in v0.9.3: a subject lost against its ground and black text lost on red (items 1, 3), an issuer without its mark (item 6), an empty scene for a fun event (item 7), and an idea that suited the name but not the product, or read as something unintended (item 8). Batches 4 and 5 (6 to 4 and 8 to 2) added more in v0.9.4: a call to action only implied by the art, a look that missed the genre, a trait explained by props or text instead of gesture, a title louder than the mood, a clinical picture book, and wins that still read sparse or sterile (items 2, 5, 7, 8 and EN-3). Batch 6, infographics only on v0.9.4, went 4 to 6 against the skill: most losses were called sterile or "like a PowerPoint slide", one chaotic, one repetitive (EN-4, LAY-18, LAY-19 in v0.9.5). This pass runs once, after accuracy and clarity pass, and pushes the piece back toward energy without letting clutter, invented content or the generated look back in.

Format: **What / Check / Fix / Why / Grade**. EN-1 to EN-4 are Procedures (`evidence.md`); the Why lines say how far the cited studies go.

### EN-1. Run it after the checks, where it applies
- **What:** Run it once the blockers and the brief check pass (MUST-BE-RIGHT, CD-1, FP-3, AES-10), before the Sitting right pass and delivery. It is part of the loop, not a new round: its fixes count toward the round's three changes.
  - **Full pass:** posters, flyers, covers, packaging, web heroes and landing pages, social cards, picture books, character art, editorial illustration, and infographics of every kind: how-tos, data, process, maps, safety and fact sheets (EN-4).
  - **Light pass** (EN-2 items 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8 only): official notices, dashboards, menus, signage and timetables. Here plain, high-contrast, easy-to-scan design is the aim, not a failure of nerve: a café menu board that is balanced and reads at a distance beat the with-skill version in batch 5 (LAY-17).
  - **Skip:** forms, dense tables, legal or medical text, settings screens, accessibility-critical UI, and any piece where calm is the job. "Quieter" from the owner wins (CMD-2).
- **Check:** Is the scope (full, light, skip) written in the critique with its reason?
- **Why:** A quiet brief still needs a clear focus and chosen type; quiet is not sterile. But energy pushed onto a form or a data table costs the task (UX-1, CORE-8).

### EN-2. The checklist (yes or no on the render)
1. **Focal hook:** one element (image, word or number) leads decisively at 150-200 px wide and in the blur test, not only slightly (CORE-3, AES-2). The main subject stands off its ground in value at that size: a dark figure on a wall of the same tone fails, however good the idea (CV-10).
2. **Confident display type:** the headline is set in a display face or weight chosen for the brief, a clear step above the next level (poster scale, TYPE-2), not body type enlarged or a default sans set plainly (TYPE-9). Its loudness matches the mood: a cold, measured thriller wants a quieter, tighter title than a fun run does (TYPE-10).
3. **Purposeful colour:** one accent pushed at the focus (CV-6), or colour that codes meaning (bins, categories, routes) backed by a second cue (CV-9). Not timid greys or a palette dulled to be safe (AES-3). Text on a saturated colour (a red band, an orange button) is checked in both black and white; use the higher-contrast one (CV-10).
4. **Movement:** at least one of a diagonal, an overlap, a bold crop off the edge, a large scale contrast or a figure in action, and it serves the idea (COMP-12, COMP-20).
5. **Call to action:** if the piece asks for something (book, come, buy, scan, report), what to do and how is found within a few seconds, second only to the hook. An instruction to the reader ("wear something bright", "bring a torch") is written in the text, not only shown in the art.
6. **Identity:** an official or branded piece shows who it is from where a viewer looks for it. On an official piece (a council, service, school or venue speaking), the issuer's real mark from its file sits with the name at the top, as the official say-so, not as advertising. No file: ask for it, or leave a labelled slot for it and flag it in the critique (BRAND-4). Never an invented crest, and not a bare text header with no character either: an official notice without its mark reads as amateur, or as a children's project (BRAND-8).
7. **Mood and role:** the warmth matches the brief: welcoming means warm light and an inhabited place, not empty or derelict. When the brief sells fun (a fête, a taster, an open day), show the activity and the people doing it; an empty scene undersells the event. The mood is judged against what the work is going for, not as "more positive is better". A character's role or trait reads from gesture and pose first, with one or two cues at most: not a pile of props, labels or explanatory text (CHAR-6). For young children: few, big, simple shapes, with life in them: picture books want energy and warmth, not clean, clinical art (ILL-17).
8. **Concept fit:** the visual idea suits the product's category and its sensory cues (taste, warmth, texture, smell), not only the literal name. A coffee named after a harbour should still read as coffee, not as soap or fabric softener: sea blues and wave motifs say "fresh and clean" before they say "roasted". Then look for readings nobody intended: a lone object can tell a story of its own (one shoe at the foot of a climbing wall can read as an accident). Name the category in a few words and check the piece against them in the thumbnail. The look also fits the genre and its tradition: a farm jam looks traditional, not like a tech product, and cover styling tells a classic from pulpy young-adult fiction (AES-11, LAY-16).
- **Fix:** One or two changes at a time (AES-10), by restyling what the brief gives: scale, crop, angle, weight, colour, light. Compare old and new side by side for the brief (AES-9).
- **Why:** Item 8: packaging colour, imagery and labels set people's expectations of how a food or drink will taste, and those expectations shape what they report tasting [S334]; work that fits its category is liked more until novelty is earned (AES-11, [S232]). Item 3: on mid reds and oranges, black and white text come out close on the WCAG 2 ratio, and a perceptual contrast model and many readers favour white [S335]; on the red header of one batch 3 infographic, white measured 4.74:1 and black 4.43:1. In eye-tracking studies of print ads, the picture captured attention whatever its size, and attention to text grew with its surface size [S333]. A designed, structured image raised attention and liking, while dense pixel-level detail lowered them [S332]. These are ads seen in magazines, one study each: a direction, not a law.

### EN-3. Guardrails: energy without slop
- **What:** Energy comes from structure (shapes, scale, arrangement, one strong colour), not from surface detail (texture, noise, glow, many hues, extra objects) [S332]. After the pass:
  - still one idea, and nothing added only to be loud (CORE-8, CMD-1 check 3);
  - no added story, text, badges or claims (AES-10);
  - no over-render: grain, speckle, noisy micro-detail, airbrushed gloss, glow on every edge, sparkles, bokeh, or the glowing, over-detailed chocolate-box painting; aim for clean, concise and dynamic (ILL-11);
  - no outline stroke round a subject and no glow or halo round text to separate them: fix the values (CV-10);
  - no generic generated layout: a centred hero in a radial burst, every surface textured, floating particles, ribbons and seals nobody asked for, fake small print (UI-7, TYPE-9).
- **Check:** The auto check has no new FAIL; the five tests still pass; the piece reads in the thumbnail at least as well as before. Then judge it honestly against the left column below: even the skill's wins in batch 5 were called sparse (a library infographic) or "academically tight but bland and sterile" (a play poster). If it reads that way, one more item from EN-2 is due, not a pass.
- **The failure pair.** Both ends fail; aim for the middle column.

| Sterile (over-corrected) | Aim | Slop (under-corrected) |
|---|---|---|
| Plain sans at body weight, a timid headline | One display face, a big step in size | Many effects, outlines, gradients on type |
| Greys or one dull hue | One accent at the focus, or a colour code | Every hue at full saturation |
| Static, centred, all elements one size | One hero, the rest clearly smaller | Everything big and competing |
| Empty flat ground, clip-art icons | Simple shapes in one drawn style | Grain, speckle, glossy rendered icons |
| The action buried in small text | The action second in the hierarchy | The action shouting among fake badges |

- **Grade:** Procedure. Internal evidence (`examples/blind-test/RESULTS.md`), one judge, one sample per arm: batches 1 and 2, 10 to 10, no clear result; batches 3 to 5, with this pass, 22 to 7 with one tie (one-sided sign test p ≈ 0.004), but not independent of the rule-writing (each batch's rules came from the owner's notes on the one before; batch 5, on unchanged v0.9.3, is the cleanest replication, 8 to 2, p = 0.055), and the maker saw both arms. All 50 briefs: 32 to 17 with one tie (p = 0.022), meeting the bar set in advance; batch 6 (infographics, v0.9.4) then went 4 to 6, so all 60 stand at 36 to 23 with one tie (p ≈ 0.059), just short of the 37-of-60 bar set before it. The sterile-versus-slop pattern and the v0.9.3 and v0.9.4 additions come from the owner's notes, not from a measured effect. Ad studies [S332, S333]: partly supported (one large study each, ads only). Related: "most advanced, yet acceptable" (AES-11, [S232]); lower perceived complexity helps first impressions [S35].

### EN-4. Infographics: warm and rich, never a slide, never chaos
- **What:** Judge an infographic as its commissioner would: the information right first, then appeal. It gets the full pass. Before the prompt, write its art direction in one sentence: a style with personality, an emotional register that fits the issuer (a bakery cosy and crafted, a health service calm and reassuring, a fun run bright and loud) and what each picture shows. "Clean" means easy to read, not bare: flat boxes, stock icons and a default sans on white read as a PowerPoint slide. Aim for visual richness that carries information: drawn scenes, objects with character, a colour world, life in the drawing (not in noise or texture, EN-3).
- **Check, busy but not chaotic:** (a) one illustration style throughout; (b) one reading path (numbers, arrows or a trail) a stranger follows first time; (c) one hero and at most two secondary focal points in view; (d) every picture says something about its step or fact, none is filler, and no two panels repeat (LAY-18); (e) text on calm ground (LAY-15), big enough for the distance (LAY-7); (f) given numbers or a route are drawn in code (LAY-19). Then two honest tests. Could it pass for a default slide template? Sterile: fix it. Does any area have competing styles, hues or focal points with no order? Chaotic: fix it.
- **Fix:** Sterile: add the scene, person or object the step is about; warm the palette; push the display type. Chaotic: one style, number the path, cut the third focal point.
- **Why:** Batch 6 lost 4 to 6: a bar chart "accurate but sterile like a PowerPoint slide" (S63), a timeline "too basic" against one with vibes and a good amount of visual busyness (S65), a fact sheet "matter of fact and sterile" against one with emotional direction (S66), a safety sheet practical but not eye-pleasing (S61), and one "busy and visually chaotic" (S68). The wins were cleaner, with punchier type and pictures (S59, S60, S67).
- **Grade:** Procedure. Internal evidence, one judge; untested until batch 7 is judged.
