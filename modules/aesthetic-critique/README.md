# Module: aesthetic-critique

Judging whether a piece is *good*, not only *correct*. The other modules catch errors and weak structure; this one helps with energy, atmosphere, distinctiveness and fit to intent. Load it with `core/` once the blockers are known, or whenever the brief asks for mood, character or a particular feel. It adds no new blockers.

Three working rules for this module:
- **Interpretation, labelled as such.** Every aesthetic finding names a visible feature, the effect it has, and what to do. "It feels flat" is not a finding; "the sky and the figure share one value group, so nothing comes forward" is.
- **Compare, don't score.** The verdict comes from side-by-side comparison against the brief (AES-9), not from adding up dimension notes. Dimension notes are evidence for the comparison.
- **Taste calls go to the owner of the work.** Where two readings are both defensible, say so and ask.

Format: **What / Check / Fix / See** (the existing rules cited under *See* carry the sources). Procedures AES-0, AES-9 and AES-10 frame the loop; AES-1 to AES-8 are diagnostic prompts.

---

### AES-0. Intent model (step 0, before judging)
- **What:** Write down, in one short block: purpose, audience, mood, style, the focal experience (what someone should feel or notice first), degree of polish wanted, and must-keeps. Mark each line **[brief]** if stated, or **[inferred]** if you guessed it; inferred lines are uncertain and are the first thing to confirm with the owner.
- **Check:** Can you say what the piece is *for* in one sentence? Is any judgement below resting on an inferred line?
- **Fix:** If mood or polish is inferred and the call matters, ask before revising. A rough look may be the point.
- **See:** CD-1, CD-2, `core/brand-intake.md`.

**Intentional or accidental?** Before calling anything a flaw, ask:
1. What effect does this choice create?
2. Does that effect serve the intent?
3. Is there evidence it is deliberate (repeated, consistent, matches the brief or style)?
4. If deliberate: does it work?
5. If accidental: would changing it help, or is it harmless?

A deliberate break that works is recorded as a deliberate exception, not fixed.

---

### AES-1. Composition and rhythm
- **What:** How the eye travels: entry point, path, pauses, exit. Repetition with variation in shape, interval and direction gives rhythm; even spacing everywhere gives monotony.
- **Check:** Trace the first three places the eye lands (blur test). Do intervals between repeated elements vary? Is there a rest area?
- **Fix:** Vary one interval or size; open one quiet area; point a directional line at the focal area.
- **See:** ILL-1, LAY-3, CORE-11, ILL-8.

### AES-2. Emphasis
- **What:** What wins attention, and by how much. Too little difference reads as timid; everything emphasised reads as noise.
- **Check:** Is the winner the AES-0 focal experience? Is its lead decisive at thumbnail size, or only slight?
- **Fix:** Increase the gap: lift the focal area or quieten its nearest rival, rarely both.
- **See:** CORE-1, CORE-3, ILL-2, TYPE-7.

### AES-3. Colour intelligence
- **What:** Whether colour builds mood and meaning, not only harmony: temperature, saturation range, one accent with a job, colour relationships across the frame.
- **Check:** Name the mood the palette gives. Does it match AES-0? Is saturation spread evenly (often dull) or concentrated where it matters?
  This is an interpretation for this piece, not a colour-emotion law (`core/evidence.md`).
- **Fix:** Shift temperature of the light or shadow family; mute the large areas so one accent can carry.
- **See:** CORE-2, CORE-6, ILL-4, ILL-9.

### AES-4. Shape language and silhouette
- **What:** The family of shapes (round, angular, long, blocky) and whether it says something about the subject. A consistent language reads as designed; a mixed one reads as assembled.
- **Check:** Fill the main shapes in black: do they read, and do they share a character that fits the mood?
- **Fix:** Push the dominant shape type further; remove or restyle the shapes that break it.
- **See:** ILL-3, ILL-8, CHAR-8.

### AES-5. Craft
- **What:** Care at the level people notice: edge control, transitions, spacing, finish appropriate to the intended polish. Over-finish can be a fault when the brief wants energy.
- **Check:** At full size, where does finish drop or jump? Does polish match the AES-0 polish line?
- **Fix:** Bring the weakest visible area up to the level of the rest, or deliberately roughen an over-smooth area.
- **See:** ILL-5, CORE-4, CORE-5, CORE-9.

### AES-6. Originality and specificity
- **What:** Whether the piece could only belong to this brief. Generic work uses the first idea, stock poses, default palettes and centred everything.
- **Check:** Swap the title for another subject: would the image still fit? If yes, it is generic. Name one specific detail drawn from the brief's subject or audience.
- **Fix:** Replace one generic element with a specific one taken from the brief (place, object, period, gesture).
- **See:** ILL-11, UI-7, TYPE-9, CD-3, BRAND-7.

### AES-7. Emotional and narrative charge
- **What:** Whether something is happening or felt: a moment, a tension, an implied before or after, a mood strong enough to name.
- **Check:** Say the feeling in one word without looking at the brief. Does it match AES-0's mood line?
- **Fix:** Strengthen one cue that carries the feeling: light direction, gesture, scale contrast, weather, or a pause in the composition.
- **See:** ILL-6, ILL-7, CHAR-6.

### AES-8. Coherence and art direction
- **What:** Whether image, type, colour and finish read as one decision. Two strong parts that don't belong together lose to two plain ones that do.
- **Check:** List the style signals (era, medium, texture, type style). Do any contradict each other?
- **Fix:** Bring the outlier into the shared direction, or remove it.
- **See:** CD-2, CD-5, BRAND-6, CORE-9, CHAR-5.

---

### AES-9. Pairwise judgement (the verdict)
- **What:** Judge two or more versions side by side: "Which is stronger *for this brief*, and why?" Report:
  1. the strongest difference between them;
  2. the decisions that caused it;
  3. what the preferred one costs (what it gives up);
  4. strength of preference: **strong** or **marginal**;
  5. "none is good enough" when that is the honest answer.
- **Check:** Compare at the real viewing size and as thumbnails. Swap left and right and judge again: if the choice flips, the preference is marginal (judges, human or model, favour position [S146]). Don't average dimension notes into a score; people are more consistent comparing than rating [S143, S144].
- **Fix:** If marginal, keep the version that breaks fewer rules and preserves more of the intent, and say it was close.
- **See:** CD-3, `core/critique-template.md`.

### AES-10. Revision with regression check
- **What:** Change one or two aesthetic things at a time, then compare old and new side by side (AES-9). Decide **keep**, **refine** or **revert**, and state the cost.
- **Check:** After each change re-run the measured checks: contrast, type size, collisions, safe area, anatomy. A change that improves mood but breaks a blocker is a regression, not a win.
- **Fix:** Revert what regressed; refine what helped partly; stop when the next change is a coin-flip.
- **See:** `core/critique-loop.md` (round limits), CD-6.

---

## Using it in a critique

1. AES-0 before anything else; flag inferred lines.
2. Measured findings first (other modules, tools). These decide blockers.
3. Pick the 2 or 3 dimensions (AES-1 to 8) that explain the biggest gap from intent. Diagnose the cause, not the symptom: "generic" is a symptom; "default centred layout, even lighting, no specific detail" is a cause.
4. Propose at most two aesthetic changes, make them, then AES-9 and AES-10.

Evidence quality, weakest to best: a general adjective; a named visible feature; a named feature plus its effect plus a side-by-side showing the change helped.

Measured and interpreted findings can disagree (a safer palette passes every check and kills the mood). Don't resolve that toward neatness by default; state the trade-off and let the owner decide.
