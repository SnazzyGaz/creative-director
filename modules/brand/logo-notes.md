# Logo notes: types, moods, clever marks, trends, cases, reading

Reference for `logo.md` (LOGO-1 to LOGO-16). How marks represent ideas (abstraction, reduction, gesture, letterforms, construction): `mark-making.md`; double readings and mashups in depth: `clever-marks.md`. Load when choosing a type, mapping a mood, looking for a clever move or checking a trend. Famous marks are named as cited examples only: describe them, never draw or reproduce them (BRAND-4, BRAND-8). Grades follow `core/evidence.md`.

## Types (LOGO-9)

Mollerup sorts marks into name marks, abbreviation (letter) marks and picture marks, figurative or not [SRC-343]; the big compendiums sort by visual form (circles, crosses, letters, animals...) [SRC-344, SRC-345, SRC-346, SRC-347]. Browse those categories during thumbnails to see what is crowded.

| Type | What it is | Use when | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | the name, drawn or set with one ownable move | short or distinctive name; new brand; little media spend | needs custom detail or it is just a font |
| Lettermark / monogram | initials or one letter | long name; app icon; heritage | letters alone carry little meaning; common pairs are crowded |
| Pictorial | a recognisable thing | the thing is ownable and lasting | literal clip art (BRAND-9); category clichés |
| Abstract | a non-figurative form | many products; global; time and money to teach it | means nothing until learned [SRC-342] |
| Mascot | a character | story, children, community | ages fast; poor at 16 px: pair with a simple head or initial |
| Emblem / badge | name inside a shape | heritage, clubs, crafts, fixed settings | small text dies at small sizes; needs a small version |
| Combination | symbol plus wordmark | the default; lets the symbol be learned | the parts must share one style (LOGO-5) |
| Dynamic / responsive | a system that changes by use or size | culture, media, platforms | needs a constant (LOGO-14) |

## Moods: how form carries a quality (LOGO-7)

**Evidence first.** Round vs angular: round reads soft and comfortable, angular hard and durable; the effect needs people to picture the product and fades under distraction (partly supported) [SRC-360]. Sound and shape: most people match spiky shapes to sharp sounds and rounded shapes to soft ones (well supported as a sound-shape match; using it for name and mark fit is craft) [SRC-367]. Symmetry: asymmetric marks are more arousing and suit exciting brands (partly supported) [SRC-362]. Implied motion: a still mark that suggests movement raises engagement, more when the motion fits the brand (partly supported) [SRC-365]. Broken or incomplete letters: more innovative, less trustworthy (partly supported) [SRC-363]. Natural, harmonious, moderately elaborate marks are recognised and liked best (partly supported) [SRC-359]. Colour: warm saturated hues read exciting and blue competent in one set of studies (partly supported) [SRC-366], but colour-emotion claims are weak overall: choose colour for distinctiveness first (`core/evidence.md`, BRAND myths).

**Craft map** (practitioner consensus, not tested as a table). Font picks: the mood tags in `fonts/CATALOGUE.md`.

| Quality | Shape and line | Angle, rhythm, weight | Type and colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trust | closed, stable forms; firm base | symmetry; even rhythm; steady weight | humanist sans or sturdy serif; calm, few colours |
| Speed | tapers, cut corners, open shapes | forward slant (about 8 to 15 degrees); horizontal motion | italics, wide sans; high contrast |
| Warmth | round terminals, soft curves, a hand-drawn line | loose rhythm; medium weight | lowercase, rounded or soft serif; warm hues |
| Luxury | thin hairlines, a fine monogram | symmetry; generous space | high-contrast serif, tracked caps; black, white or one metal |
| Play | blobs, characters, bounce | irregular baseline; varied sizes | rounded, chunky; bright, several colours |
| Craft | hand marks, stamps, engraved lines | slight irregularity | slab or script; ink colours; a clean small version |
| Heritage | badge, crest, ribbon, seal | symmetry; centred | serif, slab, blackletter; deep colours; avoid fake "est." dates |
| Precision | monoline, grid-built, sharp joins | exact repeats; even weight | geometric or grotesk sans; one colour |
| Rebellion | broken, cut, stencilled, cropped | asymmetry; clash | blackletter, stencil, distressed; clashing colour (costs trust [SRC-363]) |

## Clever marks (LOGO-8)

Famous examples, cited; describe them, never copy them. More techniques, process, failure modes and the evidence on why people like them: `clever-marks.md`.

- **Negative-space double read:** an arrow in the gap between the E and the x of FedEx, by Lindon Leader at Landor, with custom letters drawn so the arrow fits without distorting them [SRC-354, SRC-355]; a peacock whose body is the white gap between six coloured feathers (NBC, Steff Geissbuhler at Chermayeff & Geismar); a panda completed by the white around it (WWF, Peter Scott, later Landor); an ice hockey team's W, whale tail and H in one form (Peter Good) [SRC-354].
- **Merged meanings:** two overlapping circles whose overlap colour was tuned to look like a real mix (Mastercard, Pentagram 2016) [SRC-351].
- **A hidden letter:** a retailer's two arrow shapes leave its initial C in the gap between them (Carrefour) [SRC-354].
- **One gesture:** a symbol meant to be drawn in one line by anyone (Airbnb's Bélo, DesignStudio 2014) [SRC-372].
- **A meaningful constraint:** a museum W that is one zigzag line reshaped to fit each layout (Whitney, Experimental Jetset 2013) [SRC-352]; a lab's monogram and a glyph for each of its 23 research groups, all on one seven-by-seven grid (MIT Media Lab, Pentagram 2014) [SRC-350].
- **When cleverness hurts:** the trick needs explaining; it fights the plain read; it is forced (objects jammed into counters [SRC-358]); it has a rude or hateful reading (parodies hit the Bélo at launch [SRC-372]; the London 2012 mark drew swastika and other jibes [SRC-373]); or the break signals unreliability (broken type [SRC-363]).

## Trends 2024 to 2026 (LOGO-10)

Labelled as trends, from LogoLounge's yearly reports on tens of thousands of submitted logos; the author calls them a report, not a recommendation [SRC-358].
- **2024:** flat boxes, corner chops, pixel drops, liquid bridges, smileys, stickers, centre points, radar sweeps, four-point "dazzle" stars as the AI motif, organic multicolour gradients, pillow shapes; logos stepping back inside wider systems [SRC-356].
- **2025:** sharps, smokies, coves, scalers, crossovers, type melts, blur tails, poly grids, border lands; a split between ultra-chroma AI-futurist colour and muted earthy tones; more contrast for small screens [SRC-357].
- **2026:** slip knots, slant breaks, speed-line "piano" stripes (an 80s Olympics echo), faders, cracks, echo arcs, colorforms, open axes, tri-links; identities as toolkits (Koto's generative system for MassiveMusic); active gradient grounds; slants and italics; saturated motifs: script L monograms, feathers, pennants, watermelons [SRC-358].

**What dates fast** (craft): effects tied to a moment (a specific gradient, glass, pillow shading, the AI star), and any motif on a saturation list. **What lasts:** plain geometry and drawn letters, which is why many marks from 1940 to 1980 still look current [SRC-346]. Put trends in colour, motion and pattern, which can change.

## Cases: why marks succeed or fail (LOGO-12)

- **Dropped the known asset:** a juice pack redesign (2009) lost its familiar image; sales reportedly fell about 20% and the old pack returned [SRC-370]. A retailer's 2010 logo swap was reversed within a week [SRC-371]. Lesson: list and keep the assets [SRC-120].
- **Backlash is not the verdict:** the Bélo was mocked at launch and survived with consistent use [SRC-372]; London 2012 was attacked in 2007, then accepted once seen across venues and tickets [SRC-373].
- **A deliberate break:** Jaguar's 2024 identity swapped heritage cues for a rounded wordmark and bright colour, keeping the leaper as a maker's mark; reaction was heavily negative and the outcome is open [SRC-374]. Loyal customers resist such shifts [SRC-364].
- **Evolve towards the core:** Mastercard's 2016 redesign kept the circles and cleared everything else [SRC-351]. MIT Media Lab moved from a generative mark to a fixed one with a system [SRC-350].
