# "May Specials" A-board: first-principles brief (self-judged)
Trigger (FP-1): the owner of the work asked for it "from scratch, with an aesthetic pass".

## Facts as transcribed (FP-3), word for word from the perspective-corrected photo (original.jpg)
- MAY SPECIALS! / Bigger Flavor. Better Times.
- $16 Queso Popper SMASH. SPICY • LOADED • BOLD. "Double smash patties with pepper jack cheese, jalapeño poppers, bacon, grilled green chiles, fresh pico de gallo & jalapeño cheese sauce on a toasted brioche bun."

- Finish it off with a shake!
- CHURRO Shake $12. "Hand-spun vanilla shake with cinnamon sugar, caramel drizzle & whipped cream."

- PATTY'S DINER (badge logo at the bottom and on the cups).

## Fact check (FP-3)
- The prices are consistent wherever they appear. No year, address, hours or phone number on the original, and none added.
- "Butterfinger" is a third-party trademark. It's set as plain text only, with no logo and no styled wordmark.
- Patty's Diner logo: no official file was supplied. The only source is a 330×110 px glare-affected region of a phone photo, and tracing a shaded multi-colour badge from that would be a guess. So the name is set in plain type ("PATTY'S DINER") with no imitation of the badge shape. **Owner: please supply the logo file.**

## Step back (FP-2)
1. What: an A-board sidewalk sign, about 24×36 in portrait, read by people walking past, first at 3–5 m for a second or two, then at about 1 m if they stop [brief].
2. For: passers-by and regulars on the street [inferred].
3. One job: make someone walking by want the burger and come in. They should know it's a May special and what it costs [inferred].
4. Feeling: hungry, sunny, desert-diner fun [inferred from the theme].
5. Facts: listed above.
6. Must survive: all four items, their prices, descriptions and taglines, the tagline, "Finish it off with a shake!", and the Patty's Diner name. Incidental in the old version: equal visual weight everywhere, 11 different type treatments, busy desert background art, logo cups, tiny body copy (about 1.5% of the board height, unreadable beyond 1 m).
7. Success: from 4 m the reader gets "MAY SPECIALS", the burger, "$16" and "Patty's Diner". At 1 m every description reads. Body text cap height is at least 0.4% of the viewing distance.

## Brief (FP-4)
A 24×36 in sidewalk A-board for Patty's Diner, read in two passes: the 4 m glance and the 1 m stop. One job: the burger and $16 sell the stop. Feel: warm desert-diner fun, not clutter. Must survive: every item, price, description, tagline and the name. Free to change: layout, art and the type system. Done when the headline, the burger and its price read at 4 m and the descriptions read at 1 m. Open: logo file, and whether the shakes also need to read from a distance.

## Concepts (FP-5); thumbnails in concepts.png
- A "Hero and strip": one big burger leads, with the tots and the two shakes in a lower ticket strip; two reading distances.
- B "Menu board": a typographic list of four equal rows with small photos down the side, read top to bottom like a menu.
- C "2×2 tiles": four equal photo tiles under the title, with the price on each tile.
- Structure test: lead element, how the space is split, and reading order all differ. Pass.
- Recommend A, because the brief's one job is the burger at 4 m, and only A gives it the size to read from there. B is the most legible at 1 m but has nothing that reads at 4 m. C is fair to every item but makes all four small. The owner had said to go ahead without stopping for sign-off, so A was built.
