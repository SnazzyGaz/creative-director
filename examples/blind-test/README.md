# Blind test kit: with and without the skill

**Batches 1 to 8, judged by the owner of the work: 59 to 40 for the skill with 1 tie over 100 briefs (sign test p = 0.035), meeting the 59-of-100 bar set in advance below, so a clear overall result, by one win.** Batch 8, thirty briefs over six themes against v0.9.6, went 20 to 10 (p = 0.049); the skill took covers, packaging and logos 4 to 1, characters and picture books 3 to 2, and lost screens 2 to 3. At 70 briefs it was 39 to 30 with 1 tie (p = 0.17), short of the 43-of-70 bar. At 60 briefs it was 36 to 23 with 1 tie (p = 0.059), one short of the 37-of-60 bar. At 50 briefs it was 32 to 17 with 1 tie (p = 0.022), which met the 32-of-50 bar. Batches 1 and 2, before the energy pass, tied 10 to 10. Batches 3 to 5, after it, went 22 to 7 with 1 tie (p ≈ 0.004): batch 3 8 to 1, batch 4 6 to 4, and batch 5, a replication on the unchanged v0.9.3 skill, 8 to 2 (p = 0.055). Batch 6, infographics only against v0.9.4, went 4 to 6: the losses were called sterile or like a slide, one repetitive and one chaotic, and neither side drew its charts to scale. Batch 7, infographics against v0.9.5, went 3 to 7: v0.9.5 overcorrected into over-illustrated, unbalanced pieces with charts that looked pasted on, though its charts were the accurate ones; v0.9.6 answers it. Those later batches are not independent of the rule-writing, the maker saw both arms, and there is one judge, so this is internal evidence of one owner's taste, not research. The skill's lead is almost all in pictures; on posters it is about even, on screens and infographics behind (5 to 7 and 7 to 13). Full write-up, the owner's reasons, the off-white and chart findings and the gaps found: [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md). Keys 1 to 8 are published here. Nothing has been shown to outside models yet.

This tests one narrow question: given the same brief and the same image model, does a prompt written by following this skill give a piece that people would rather hand to a client than the plain brief does? It does **not** test the critique loop, type set in code, or any of the Python checks.

## How the images were made

- **Briefs.** 100 briefs in eight batches. All 100, with both prompts word for word, are in [`briefs.json`](briefs.json).
  - **Batch 1 (B1-B8):** reused from earlier examples, written before this test: 3 posters, 3 illustrations, 2 UI screens.
  - B1 jazz night poster and B4 fox book cover come from [`../baseline-comparison/`](../baseline-comparison/).
  - B2, B3, B5, B6, B7 and B8 come from the frozen briefs in [`../aesthetic-benchmark/reference-set/sources/briefs.md`](../aesthetic-benchmark/reference-set/sources/briefs.md) (01, 02, 03, 12, 05, 06).
  - **Batch 2 (B9-B20):** written for this test on 3 Nov 2026, after the owner asked for more pairs to judge, and before any of their images were made: 4 posters and flyers (B9-B12), 4 illustrations (book cover, editorial, character, picture-book scene: B13-B16), 2 UI screens (B17, B18), a packaging label (B19) and an infographic (B20). Plain, realistic briefs with invented names; some give the exact copy, some leave it open (B10's headline, B19's label copy). Made the same way as batch 1.
  - **Batch 3 (B21-B30):** written for this test on 3 Nov 2026, after the batch 1 and 2 result and after the energy pass was added, and before any of their images were made: 2 posters (B21, B22), an infographic (B23), 2 picture-book pieces (cover B24, spread B25), a coffee bag (B26), a web hero (B27), 2 official notices (B28, B29) and a character concept (B30). Each brief gives its exact copy, since open copy pushed earlier with-skill prompts into placeholders. Made the same way, one sheet per brief.
  - **Batch 4 (B31-B40):** written for this test on 3 Nov 2026, after the batch 3 result and the v0.9.3 rules, and before any of their images were made. Half probe the gaps still open after batch 3: 2 covers where the title and the main image could compete (novel B31, picture book B32), 2 character concepts whose defining trait has to show (B33, B34), and 2 official pieces with an issuer identity (notice B35, public infographic B36). The rest spread across a poster (B37), a jam label (B38), a web hero (B39) and a picture-book spread (B40). All names are invented; each brief gives its exact copy.
  - **Batch 5 (B41-B50):** written for this test on 3 Nov 2026, against the same, unchanged v0.9.3 skill, and before any of their images were made. A clean replication of batch 4: no rules were added after it, and the method is the same. A poster (B41), a tea box (B42), a web hero (B43), a public infographic (B44), an official notice (B45), a picture-book cover (B46), a character concept (B47), a café menu board (B48), a novel cover (B49) and a picture-book spread (B50). All names are invented; each brief gives its exact copy; B44 and B45 describe the issuer's mark in words, as in batch 4.
  - **Batch 6 (B51-B60):** written for this test on 3 Nov 2026, against the newly released v0.9.4 skill, and before any of their images were made. A focused test on infographics only, the category batches 3 to 5 left clear but plain: two public how-tos (register to vote B51, home blood pressure B52), a bar chart with given numbers (B53), a 48-hour process timeline (B54), a safety sheet (B55), a children's water cycle (B56), a bin-sorting guide (B57), a fun-run route map (B58), a four-panel comic-strip how-to (B59) and a nutrition fact sheet with given percentages charted as bars (B60). Five portrait, five landscape. All names are invented; each brief gives its exact copy; B51, B52, B55 and B57 describe the issuer's mark in words, as in batches 4 and 5.
  - **Batch 7 (B61-B70):** written for this test on 3 Nov 2026, against the newly released v0.9.5 skill (EN-4, LAY-18, LAY-19 and the chart tool), and before any of their images were made. Infographics again: three bar charts with given numbers (library loans B61, reading results B63, council tax B69), a route map (B62), a recycling process (B64), a comic-strip how-to (B65), a safety sheet (B66), a timeline (B67), a children's life cycle (B68) and a wildlife fact sheet with a two-bar chart (B70). All names are invented; each brief gives its exact copy and numbers.
  - **Batch 8 (B71-B100):** written for this test on 3 Nov 2026, against the newly released v0.9.6 skill, and before any of their images were made. Thirty briefs, five in each of six themes, to test the skill outside infographics: covers (novels, albums, a popular-science and a chapter book, B71-B75), packaging (coffee, hot sauce, chocolate, honey, shampoo, B76-B80), screens (web heroes, app screens, a pricing section, B81-B85), characters and creatures (B86-B90), picture-book spreads (B91-B95) and logos (a single mark plus the wordmark on a plain ground, B96-B100). All names are invented; each brief gives its exact copy, screens list every word on them, and B99 names a warm cream ground (CV-11's warm exception). Each brief carries a `theme` field.
  - **Issuer marks in batches 4 to 7.** A real client would supply a logo file, but the image model can't take one, so each official brief describes the issuer's mark in words ("a deep teal circle containing a white stylised oak leaf…") as part of the brief. Both arms get that description: the without side in the brief as sent, the with side as the supplied mark to reproduce once, at the top, with the issuer's name (BRAND-4, EN-2 item 6). Neither side invents a crest or leaves a slot, so the sheet compares how each uses the same identity, not whether one had a logo.
- **Without the skill:** the brief text, sent as is.
- **With the skill:** a prompt written by Grok Bot following the skill at Step 0: only the brief's facts as text, a hierarchy, two typefaces at most, one light, a value plan, a small palette and a list of things not to add. Batch 3's, batch 4's and batch 5's with-skill prompts also follow the energy pass (EN-2): a focal hook, confident display type, purposeful colour, movement or scale contrast, an obvious call to action and the issuer's name on official pieces, with no added grain or texture. Batch 6's follow v0.9.4 as well: white means pure neutral white unless the brief asks otherwise, every instruction is in the text as well as the picture, each panel is checked on its own, small text is sized to read at a distance, no glow or outline round text, and a check that the piece isn't sterile. Batch 4's, 5's and 6's also follow the v0.9.3 additions: the subject standing off its ground in value, the better of black or white text on strong colour, an idea that fits the category, and the issuer's supplied mark on official pieces; covers give the title its own clear zone, and character prompts stage the defining trait in the pose. When the brief left copy open (B4's title, B7's pitch, B10's headline, B19's label), the with-skill prompt used the brief's own words or a marked placeholder.
- **Batch 7 method change (with side only).** LAY-19 says charts and routes are drawn from the data in code, so for the five briefs with numbers or a route (B61, B62, B63, B69, B70) the with-side prompt asked for the art with an area left clear, and `tools/datagraphic.py` drew the bars or route from the brief's numbers and composited them into that area, with their labels set in code. Where the model put marks in the clear area anyway (B61; B70, where it drew its own wrong bars), that area was wiped to white before compositing. Still one image call per arm, first output kept; the other five with-side pieces and all ten without-side pieces are untouched model output. So batch 7 tests the v0.9.5 workflow, not the prompt alone, and the composited charts may be easy to spot.
- **Batch 8 method.** Back to the prompt alone: no charts, so nothing composited, and every image on both sides is untouched model output. The with-side prompts follow v0.9.6 (exact text with "No other words", a hierarchy and value plan, an idea that fits the genre, neutral white unless warmth is named, flat two-colour logo rules that must read at 16 px, an 8-point grid and 44-point targets on screens, picture-book text kept off the fold, anatomy checks). Sheets S79-S108 are shuffled with the themes interleaved (no two neighbouring sheets share a theme), and the with side is on the left on 15 of the 30 sheets, two or three times in each theme, so position can't favour one arm or one theme.
- **Same model, same effort.** Both arms used GenerateImage (Grok Bot's built-in image tool) on 3 Nov 2026, at the same aspect ratio, **one call each, first output kept**, with no edits, rerolls, retouching or type set afterwards. That is one round per arm, so the extra-round bias noted in the reference set doesn't apply here.
- B7's brief said "Photo supplied"; no photo can be given to the image model, so that phrase was dropped from both arms.

## Known biases (read before scoring)

- The with-skill prompts were written by the same bot that maintains the skill, and it saw every image while making the sheets. It must not judge.
- Batch 3 is not independent of batches 1 and 2: the energy pass was written after reading the owner's notes on them, so a batch 3 win tests that rule on new briefs, judged by the same person, not the skill as first written. Batch 4 likewise follows the owner's batch 3 notes, and its gap-probing briefs were chosen where the skill has known weaknesses.
- The judging sheets show the brief, so a judge can see that one piece sticks to the brief's words and the other adds slogans, signs or extra facts. That's part of what the skill is for, but it makes the with-skill piece easier to spot.
- Image models follow long prompts unevenly; one sample per arm per brief is noisy. `score.py` runs a one-sided sign test over the briefs that weren't ties, for all briefs together and for each batch. With no ties, a clear result (p < 0.05) needs 15 of 20 for batches 1 and 2, 7 of 8 in batch 1 alone, 10 of 12 in batch 2 alone, 9 of 10 in batch 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7 alone, or **20 of 30 in batch 8 alone** (p = 0.049; 19 of 30 gives p = 0.10). Overall it needs 20 of 30 once batch 3 is in, 26 of 40 once batch 4 is in, 32 of 50, 37 of 60 and 43 of 70 after batches 5 to 7, and **59 of 100 once batch 8 is in** (p = 0.044; 58 gives p = 0.067). A single theme in batch 8 has only five pairs, so it needs 5 of 5 (p = 0.031) and is read as a pointer, not a result; ties lower the count needed but also the briefs counted. Anything less is no clear result.
- **Repeats.** Batch 1 shows each pair twice with the sides swapped, to catch position bias. A human judge recognises the repeat, so it adds nothing for them: batches 2 to 8 have **one sheet per brief**, with the side picked at random per sheet, and batch 1's two sheets are scored as one judgement per pair (agreeing picks, or the only one filled in; disagreeing picks are a tie). Swapped repeats still make sense for outside-model judges, so `blind.py --repeat` can still build them.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| [`briefs.json`](briefs.json) | The 100 briefs (batches 1 to 8) and both prompts, word for word |
| [`blind.py`](blind.py) | Shuffles a batch's images into judging sheets (random sides, random 4-letter IDs; `--repeat` for each pair twice with sides swapped, as in batch 1) and writes that batch's hidden key |
| [`KEY-SHA256.txt`](KEY-SHA256.txt) | Hashes of the eight keys (one per batch), each committed before its batch was judged |
| `blind-test-key.json`, `-2` to `-8` | The keys for batches 1 to 8, each published after its batch was judged; `sha256sum -c KEY-SHA256.txt` passes for all eight |
| [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md) | The result, per batch and overall, with the owner's reasons and the gaps found |
| [`scoring-owner.csv`](scoring-owner.csv) | The owner's picks and strengths, with the reasons paraphrased |
| [`SCORING.md`](SCORING.md), [`scoring.csv`](scoring.csv) | The scoring form, for the owner of the work and any other judge |
| [`OUTSIDE-MODEL-PROMPT.md`](OUTSIDE-MODEL-PROMPT.md) | The prompt for outside models, to use only after the owner has scored |
| [`score.py`](score.py) | Tallies filled-in forms against the key once judging is over |
| [`whites.py`](whites.py) | Measures how white each image's whites are, per arm (CV-11); needs the images |

**Not in git:** the 200 generated images and the 108 sheets (16 in batch 1, 12 in batch 2, 10 each in batches 3 to 7, 30 in batch 8; kept out to keep the repo small). Whoever runs the test gets the sheets directly.

## Running it

1. The owner of the work scores the sheets alone, first (done for batches 1 to 8), in `scoring.csv` (or the table in `SCORING.md`). **The owner's picks weigh most**: the skill exists to make work its owner would sign off.
2. Optional: other people, then outside models with [`OUTSIDE-MODEL-PROMPT.md`](OUTSIDE-MODEL-PROMPT.md). Every judge is reported separately; model picks never outweigh the owner's.
3. Publish the keys next to `KEY-SHA256.txt`, check their hashes (`sha256sum -c`), run `python3 examples/blind-test/score.py --key blind-test-key.json --key blind-test-key-2.json --key blind-test-key-3.json --key blind-test-key-4.json --key blind-test-key-5.json --key blind-test-key-6.json --key blind-test-key-7.json --key blind-test-key-8.json scoring-*.csv`, and write up the result, whatever it is (done for batches 1 to 8: [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md)).
