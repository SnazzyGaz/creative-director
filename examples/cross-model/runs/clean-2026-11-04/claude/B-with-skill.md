### Changes, ranked

1. **The lighthouse is the focal point, not the cat (ILL-2, CORE-3, CORE-1). Measured.** The lantern area averages 198/255 in greyscale and is the only area of pure white. The cat averages 47, which is the same value as the rock right behind it (also 47). It is also only about 2% of the frame. The light rays point at the tower, so the cat has no edge or value of its own to stand out with. **Fix:** put the darkest-against-lightest contrast on the cat. For example, set its silhouette against the lit sea or the lantern beam. Calm the lantern down to a warm point with no rays, and make the cat larger in the frame. [regenerate]

2. **The keeper's small boat is missing, and the cat isn't waiting for anything (ILL-6, CD-7). Measured count.** The brief names one small boat. The picture has two tall-masted sailing ships instead. The one on the right has a broken mast and is listing, so it reads as a shipwreck. For a 4-7 audience, that suggests the keeper is lost, which works against the hope of a homecoming. The cat faces straight ahead at the rock and tower, not at either ship, so "waiting for the boat" doesn't read. **Fix:** draw one small keeper's boat with its own warm lantern, out on the water. Turn the cat in three-quarter view toward the boat, with room in front of its gaze (COMP-5), so its ears and head point at it. Remove the ships. [regenerate]

3. **There's no quiet area for the two lines of text (LAY-15, LAY-10). Measured.** I split the frame into an 8x6 grid. No cell has a tonal spread (standard deviation of greyscale values) below 23. The calmest-looking candidates, the top corners, run from 22 to about 190 in greyscale (5th to 95th percentile), and 6% of their pixels are bright stars. Neither black nor white text would hold contrast on the worst pixels. **Fix:** keep a calm band of dark storm cloud on one page, with no stars, lightning or birds, and test text contrast on its worst pixels. [regenerate]

4. **The lighthouse sits in the gutter (LAY-5). Measured.** On a spread, the fold falls at the horizontal centre. The tower's centre line is at about x=500 of 1000, so the binding would cut through the lantern and the tower. **Fix:** move the lighthouse well off centre, for example to the left page. Put the cat and the boat's line of sight across the spread, so the story runs from page to page (ILL-17). Keep the text band clear of the gutter as well. [regenerate]

5. **The generic AI look works against "a stormy sea at night" (ILL-11, CV-6, ILL-17). Interpreted.** I count five or more tells:

Seagulls flying at night in a storm add content the brief didn't ask for. The trade-off is that the colour is lively. But the brief's warmth can come from two small warm lights instead: the cottage windows and the boat's lantern. **Fix:**

   - Use a blue-grey storm with rain and big waves, and no stars.
   - Make the warm accents the strongest colour, kept small and placed at the cat and the boat.
