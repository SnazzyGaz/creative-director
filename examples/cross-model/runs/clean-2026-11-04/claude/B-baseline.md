**Short verdict:** this spread doesn't pass yet. I shrank it to about 160 px wide. At that size the eye goes straight to the lighthouse lamp and the rainbow sky. The cat is a small dark shape low on the right, and you only find it after you've looked at everything else. Nothing in the picture says "the keeper's small boat is coming home". The pieces are mostly there, but the hierarchy is upside down.

## What works

- **The cat's pose.** It sits upright with its back to us, tail tucked, very still. That is the right body language for waiting. Keep it.
- **The lit cottage windows.** Warm yellow windows in a storm read as "home, someone is missing". This is the emotional hook of the spread.
- **The cat on its own rock in the lower right third.** That's a sensible spot. It sits on a strong compositional point, and in a spread it's near where the eye ends up.
- **The sea.** Big, white-crested waves read as a storm, even at small sizes.
- **The lighthouse silhouette** is clear and simple. You can recognise it instantly.

## What must change, in order of importance

### 1. The cat is not the focal point; the lighthouse lamp is
**Problem:** The brightest point, the strongest contrast and every radiating line (the light rays, the converging rainbow clouds) all meet at the lamp. The lighthouse is also dead centre and about six times taller than the cat. The cat itself is mid-grey fur on dark rock, scattered with the same sparkles as its surroundings. In greyscale it almost disappears. So the cat fails the brief's main test.

**Fix:**
- **Give the cat the strongest value contrast in the picture.** The simplest way is to backlight it. Put the cat in the lighthouse beam, or against a pale patch of sea foam, so it reads as a crisp dark silhouette with a warm rim light along its ears and back. Another option is a lighter or more distinctive coat, such as ginger, white with patches, or a red collar. The goal is that it reads at 1 cm.
- **Make the cat bigger and bring it forward.** Roughly double its size relative to the frame, so it is clearly in the foreground and the lighthouse becomes the setting behind it.
- **Take the drama out of the lamp.** Keep a beam, because the keeper needs it to get home. But make it one or two clean beams, not a full sunburst, and point one of them across the sea toward the boat (see point 2). Then the beam directs the eye instead of competing for it.
- **Move the lighthouse off-centre,** for example to the left third. That way the image isn't anchored to a bullseye in the middle.

### 2. There is no "keeper's small boat coming home", and the cat isn't looking at anything
**Problem:** The picture has two tall-masted sailing ships, one on each edge. They read as pirate or historic vessels, not a lighthouse keeper's small boat. The one on the right is heeling over among breaking waves and reads as a shipwreck, which is the wrong message for this story and frightening for 4-7 year olds. The cat's head points up and to the left, toward the rocks and the lighthouse, not toward either ship. So the story beat "the cat is waiting for the boat" isn't shown at all.

**Fix:**
- **Remove both sailing ships.** Paint one small rowing boat or little motor boat with a single figure in a yellow oilskin. One tiny warm lantern on the bow gives it a light that rhymes with the cottage windows.
- **Put the boat where the cat is looking.** Place it out at sea, small but readable, on the same side as the cat and a little higher. That creates a clear line of sight from cat to boat. Turn the cat's head (a three-quarter back view with one ear and its cheek showing) so its gaze clearly points at the boat.
- **Let the lighthouse beam fall on the boat,** or close to it. Cat to beam to boat becomes one readable chain, and children understand it straight away: "the light is helping him get home".
- **Show "coming home",** not "lost at sea": the boat is upright and heading toward the island, with a small wake behind it. Put it in a trough between waves, not on a breaking crest.

### 3. There is no quiet area for text
**Problem:** Every part of the page is busy. The sky is full of stars, lightning and rainbow swirls. The rocks are covered in sparkles. The sea is all foam. Two lines of text would be unreadable anywhere on this spread.

**Fix:** Plan a calm area from the start, about 25-30% of the spread, in one value range with little texture. The best candidate is the upper corner opposite the lighthouse, for example the upper right if the lighthouse moves left: a dark, even, blue-grey area of storm cloud or rain, with no stars or lightning. A calm patch of dark sea in the lower foreground also works. Keep the text area clear of the gutter and the trim, and away from where the cat and boat sit.

### 4. The sky reads as a fantasy galaxy, not a stormy night
**Problem:** Rainbow nebula colours, dense starfields and neon purple lightning say "magic cosmos", not "storm at sea at night". You can't have a starry sky and a storm at the same time; storm clouds cover the stars. The saturated rainbow also pulls attention away from the cat (see point 1). For this age group, the multicoloured lightning everywhere is closer to frightening spectacle than to the cosy tension of waiting for someone to come home.

**Fix:** Use a limited night-storm palette: deep blue, slate, blue-green, plus one warm accent family (lamp, windows, boat lantern, perhaps a rim of light on the cat). Paint heavy, low clouds with rain streaks and no stars. Use at most one restrained lightning flash, placed away from the cat. If it's used, place it so it shows the boat's silhouette. Then the warm colours become the "home" signal and lead the eye from cat to boat.

### 5. Remove clutter that doesn't serve the story
**Problem:** These elements all compete for attention without adding to the story: the gull in the upper left, the scattered small birds, the glowing magenta and orange sparkles on the rocks, the extra dark shape on the far right horizon, and the railing on the far left cliff.

**Fix:** Remove them all. Paint the rocks as simple, dark, readable shapes with wet highlights. If you want a secondary detail, choose one that supports the story, such as the keeper's empty mooring post or a coil of rope next to the cat.

## Smaller notes
- **Mind the gutter.** This is a spread, but the lighthouse sits exactly in the centre, right where the binding fold goes. Moving it to one side (point 1) also fixes that. Keep the cat and boat out of the gutter too.
- **Texture style.** Once the clutter is gone, check that the rendering is consistent with the other spreads in the book. The current hyper-detailed, glowing finish is very different from typical picture-book art. Reference sheets for the cat (coat, markings, collar) will keep it recognisable from spread to spread.

## Suggested revised layout (one option)
- **Left page:** the lighthouse on the left third, with the cottage windows glowing. One beam sweeps right across the sea. Above it, the upper left is dark sky with sheets of rain.
- **Right page, foreground:** the cat, larger, on a rock in the lower right third. It is backlit by the beam or sea foam, its head turned toward the horizon.
- **Right page, mid-distance:** the small boat with a yellow-coated figure and a lantern, caught in the beam, on the cat's line of sight.
- **Text area:** the upper right, as even, dark storm cloud.

**Check before calling it done:** shrink it to thumbnail size, and also look at it in greyscale. The cat should be the first thing you see, and your eye should travel from the cat along its gaze to the boat. If the lamp or the sky still grabs the eye first, keep reducing them.
