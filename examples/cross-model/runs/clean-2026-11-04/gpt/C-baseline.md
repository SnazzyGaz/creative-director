The drawing is recognisable and the overall construction is sound. The main problem is that the box and tin aren't quite obeying the same perspective system. There are also some inconsistencies between the ellipses that make the tin feel slightly twisted rather than cylindrical.

I'm treating this as a two-point perspective drawing viewed from above, with verticals remaining vertical.

## 1. Box perspective errors



Most important

&#x20;1\. The vertical edges aren't actually vertical.



The four vertical corner edges lean in different directions.

- Left rear corner leans noticeably left as it descends.
- Front corner also leans left.
- Right rear corner leans slightly right.
- The vertical edges aren't parallel to one another.

Fix: Establish a single vertical direction and make all four box corners parallel to it. In conventional two-point perspective, these should be perfectly vertical. This is the biggest structural correction.

2\. The two sets of horizontal edges don't converge consistently.

The box has two families of receding edges, one running towards the left vanishing point and one towards the right.

Currently, the top and bottom edges don't convincingly share those directions.

In particular:

- The left-facing side has a top edge that doesn't quite agree with the corresponding bottom edge.
- The right-facing side has a similar discrepancy.
- The top plane's rear corners don't quite establish a convincing pair of vanishing directions.

Fix: Extend the four top-plane edges with a ruler (or digitally) until you can establish two vanishing points. Use those same directions for the corresponding bottom edges.

3\. The front corner is slightly inconsistent with the box's overall geometry.

The front vertical edge is leaning, and its position makes the two visible side faces feel a little uneven.

Fix: Correct the vertical first, then re-establish the front corner using the two horizontal vanishing directions. Don't independently adjust the faces.

4\. The box depth and proportions are slightly ambiguous.

The left face appears somewhat deeper than the right face, partly because the perspective convergence is inconsistent.

This isn't necessarily a dimensional error, but it makes the box feel less like a single solid object.

Fix: Reconstruct the top quadrilateral first. Then project the four corners vertically downwards to establish the bottom.

## 2. Tin ellipse and cylinder errors



Major

&#x20;5\. The top ellipse is too steeply distorted relative to the lid.



The top ellipse is reasonably well drawn as an individual shape, but its orientation and proportions don't convincingly relate to the perspective of the box's top plane.

The ellipse's long axis slopes down towards the right. That isn't inherently wrong, but it needs to be consistent with the viewing direction.

Fix: Establish the perspective of the lid first. Construct a circle in that plane, then project it into an ellipse. Don't draw the ellipse independently by eye.

6\. The top and bottom ellipses have different proportions.

This is probably the most noticeable problem with the tin itself.

The top ellipse is noticeably taller than the bottom ellipse. The bottom rim looks much flatter.

For a straight cylindrical tin, both circular ends are parallel planes. Consequently, they should produce ellipses with the same proportions and orientation under the same perspective projection.

Fix: Copy the top ellipse's proportions and orientation to the bottom ellipse. Translate it downwards along the cylinder's vertical axis.

7\. The cylinder's side edges aren't vertical.

Both sides lean slightly to the right as they descend.

They are reasonably parallel to one another, which is good, but they don't agree with the intended vertical construction of the box.

Fix: Make both cylinder sides vertical and parallel. Their horizontal separation should remain constant.

8\. The bottom ellipse doesn't convincingly connect to the cylinder walls.

The lower rim is somewhat irregular, particularly where the left wall meets the bottom curve.

The transition makes the tin look slightly pinched at its base.

Fix: Make the bottom ellipse tangent to both cylinder sides at their intersection points. Avoid abrupt changes in curvature.

9\. The ellipse curvature is slightly uneven.

The top ellipse has a reasonably smooth upper arc, but the lower arc feels a little flatter and less controlled.

An ellipse should have continuous, smooth curvature, with its flattest portions at the ends of its major axis.

Fix: Use an ellipse template or four-point construction rather than drawing the two arcs independently.

## 3. Placement and object relationships

10\. The tin is not quite centred in the lid's perspective space.

It looks approximately centred horizontally, but visual centring needs to be judged against the quadrilateral, not the image canvas.

Fix: Find the intersection of the lid's diagonals. Use that as the centre of the lid, then check the tin's projected centre against it.

One qualification: if the tin is intended to be physically centred on the lid, its circular footprint should be centred in the lid plane, not necessarily its visible top ellipse's bounding-box centre.

11\. The tin's vertical axis and the box's vertical axis disagree.

This is related to errors 1 and 7, but important enough to call out separately.

The box and tin look as though they were drawn using slightly different camera assumptions.

Fix: Use the same vertical construction for both objects. Once the box's verticals are corrected, carry that direction through the tin.

## Recommended correction order

I'd tackle the drawing in this order rather than trying to fix individual lines in isolation.

1

Reconstruct the box

Establish the two vanishing points, redraw the top quadrilateral and correct the verticals.

2

Reconstruct the tin

Establish its vertical axis and redraw the top ellipse to agree with the lid's perspective.

3

Match the ellipses

Ensure top and bottom have identical projected proportions and orientation.

4

Refine the connections

Make the cylinder walls meet the ellipses cleanly and check that the tin is centred.

## Overall assessment

The drawing's biggest weakness isn't the quality of the individual lines. It's the lack of a shared geometric construction.

The box is close enough to read, the tin is recognisable, and the corner-facing composition works. I wouldn't change the viewpoint, the object proportions or the basic arrangement.

I'd preserve the entire design and correct the underlying perspective.

One important distinction: In true two-point perspective, verticals remain vertical. If you're deliberately using a camera tilted downwards, you'd technically be moving towards three-point perspective, and the verticals would converge too. Your current drawing doesn't consistently follow either system. For this product sketch, I'd recommend conventional two-point perspective.
