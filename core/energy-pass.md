# Energy pass: eye-catch after the checks pass

**Purpose:** the loop's checks make work accurate, clear and uncluttered; on their own they can stop at "correct and plain". In the repo's blind test (`examples/blind-test/RESULTS.md`, history there) the owner of the work called many early with-skill pieces sterile, clinical or basic, and where the other side won, its notes named eye-catch, energy or an obvious call to action. This pass answers that. It runs once, after accuracy and clarity pass, and pushes the piece toward energy without letting clutter, invented content or the generated look back in. Infographics get it from structure (EN-4); screens get it too, with task clarity first (UI-12).

Format: **What / Check / Fix / Why / Grade**. EN-1 to EN-4 are Procedures (`evidence.md`); the Why lines say how far the cited studies go.

### EN-1. Run it after the checks, where it applies
- **What:** Run it once the blockers and the brief check pass (MUST-BE-RIGHT, CD-1, FP-3, AES-10), before the Sitting right pass and delivery. It is part of the loop, not a new round: its fixes count toward the round's three changes.
  - **Full pass:** posters, flyers, covers, packaging, logos, social cards, picture books, character art, editorial illustration, and screens that sell or greet: web heroes, landing pages, pricing sections, app home and summary screens (with UI-12; task clarity still wins).
  - **Structure pass** (EN-4): infographics of every kind: how-tos, data, process, maps, safety, timelines and fact sheets.
  - **Light pass** (EN-2 items 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8 only): official notices, dashboards, menus, signage and timetables, where plain, high-contrast, easy-to-scan design is the aim, not a failure of nerve (LAY-17).
  - **Skip:** forms, checkout steps, dense tables, legal or medical text, settings screens, accessibility-critical UI, and any piece where calm is the job. "Quieter" from the owner wins (CMD-2).
- **Check:** Is the scope (full, light, skip) written in the critique with its reason?
- **Why:** A quiet brief still needs a clear focus and chosen type; quiet is not sterile. But energy pushed onto a form or a data table costs the task (UX-1, CORE-8).

### EN-2. The checklist (yes or no on the render)
1. **Focal hook:** one element (image, word or number) leads decisively at 150-200 px wide and in the blur test, not only slightly (CORE-3, AES-2). The main subject stands off its ground in value at that size; e.g. a dark figure on a wall of the same tone fails, however good the idea (CV-10).
2. **Confident display type:** the headline is set in a display face or weight chosen for the brief, a clear step above the next level (poster scale, TYPE-2), not body type enlarged or a default sans set plainly (TYPE-9). Its loudness matches the mood; e.g. a cold, measured thriller wants a quieter title than a fun run (TYPE-10).
3. **Purposeful colour:** one accent pushed at the focus (CV-6), or colour that codes meaning (bins, categories, routes) backed by a second cue (CV-9). Not timid greys or a palette dulled to be safe (AES-3). Text on a saturated colour (a red band, an orange button) is checked in both black and white; use the higher-contrast one (CV-10).
4. **Movement:** at least one of a diagonal, an overlap, a bold crop off the edge, a large scale contrast or a figure in action, and it serves the idea (COMP-12, COMP-20).
5. **Call to action:** if the piece asks for something (book, come, buy, scan, report), what to do and how is found within a few seconds, second only to the hook. An instruction to the reader ("wear something bright", "bring a torch") is written in the text, not only shown in the art.
6. **Identity:** a branded or official piece shows who it is from where a viewer looks for it. On an official piece the issuer's real mark from its file sits with the name at the top, as the official say-so; no file: ask, or leave a labelled slot and flag it (BRAND-4). Never an invented crest, and not a bare text header either (BRAND-8). On packaging the brand name is a designed mark with character (BRAND-10).
7. **Mood and role:** the warmth matches what the work is going for (not "more positive is better"): welcoming means warm light and an inhabited place. When the brief sells fun, show the activity and the people doing it. A character's role or trait reads from gesture and pose first, one or two cues at most (CHAR-6). For young children: few, big, simple shapes with life in them, never clinical (ILL-17). Screens get a named mood and atmosphere too (UI-12).
8. **Concept fit:** the visual idea suits the product's category and its sensory cues (taste, warmth, texture, smell), not only the literal name; e.g. a coffee named after a harbour still reads as coffee, not fabric softener. Look for readings nobody intended (a lone shoe at a climbing wall can read as an accident). The idea goes one step past the literal, not two (BRAND-9). Name the category in a few words and check the thumbnail against them. The look fits the genre and its tradition (AES-11, LAY-16).
- **Fix:** One or two changes at a time (AES-10), by restyling what the brief gives: scale, crop, angle, weight, colour, light. Compare old and new side by side for the brief (AES-9).
- **Why:** Item 8: packaging colour, imagery and labels set people's expectations of how a food or drink will taste, and those expectations shape what they report tasting [SRC-334]; work that fits its category is liked more until novelty is earned (AES-11, [SRC-232]). Item 3: on mid reds and oranges, black and white text come out close on the WCAG 2 ratio, and a perceptual contrast model and many readers favour white [SRC-335]; In eye-tracking studies of print ads, the picture captured attention whatever its size, and attention to text grew with its surface size [SRC-333]. A designed, structured image raised attention and liking, while dense pixel-level detail lowered them [SRC-332]. These are ads seen in magazines, one study each: a direction, not a law.

### EN-3. Guardrails: energy without slop
- **What:** Energy comes from structure (shapes, scale, arrangement, one strong colour), not from surface detail (texture, noise, glow, many hues, extra objects) [SRC-332]. After the pass:
  - still one idea, and nothing added only to be loud (CORE-8, CMD-1 check 3);
  - no added story, text, badges or claims (AES-10);
  - no over-render: grain, speckle, noisy micro-detail, airbrushed gloss, glow on every edge, sparkles, bokeh, or the glowing, over-detailed chocolate-box painting; aim for clean, concise and dynamic (ILL-11);
  - no outline stroke round a subject and no glow or halo round text to separate them: fix the values (CV-10);
  - no generic generated layout: a centred hero in a radial burst, every surface textured, floating particles, ribbons and seals nobody asked for, fake small print (UI-7, TYPE-9).
- **Check:** The auto check has no new FAIL; the five tests still pass; the piece reads in the thumbnail at least as well as before. Then judge it honestly against the left column below (even wins have been called "academically tight but bland and sterile"); if it reads that way, one more item from EN-2 is due, not a pass.
- **The failure pair.** Both ends fail; aim for the middle column.

| Sterile (over-corrected) | Aim | Slop (under-corrected) |
|---|---|---|
| Plain sans at body weight, a timid headline | One display face, a big step in size | Many effects, outlines, gradients on type |
| Greys or one dull hue | One accent at the focus, or a colour code | Every hue at full saturation |
| Static, centred, all elements one size | One hero, the rest clearly smaller | Everything big and competing |
| Empty flat ground, clip-art icons | Simple shapes in one drawn style | Grain, speckle, glossy rendered icons |
| The action buried in small text | The action second in the hierarchy | The action shouting among fake badges |

- **Grade:** Procedure. Internal evidence (`examples/blind-test/RESULTS.md`), one judge, one sample per arm, and the maker saw both arms. Batches 1 and 2 (no pass): 10 to 10. With it, all 100 briefs stand at 59 to 40 with one tie (one-sided sign test p = 0.035), meeting the 59-of-100 bar set in advance by one win; batch 8 (v0.9.6, six themes) went 20 to 10 (p = 0.049). Not independent of the rule-writing: each batch's rules came from the owner's notes on the one before, and the infographic batches 6 and 7 went 4 to 6 and 3 to 7 before EN-4. The sterile-versus-slop pattern comes from the owner's notes, not a measured effect. Ad studies [SRC-332, SRC-333]: partly supported (one large study each, ads only). Related: "most advanced, yet acceptable" (AES-11, [SRC-232]); lower perceived complexity helps first impressions [SRC-35].

### EN-4. Infographics: energy from structure, not from illustration
- **What:** Judge an infographic as its commissioner would: the information right first, then appeal. Its energy comes from the layout, the type, the colour structure and a focal chart or diagram, not from piling on pictures. Aim for compact, balanced and information-dense, in one coherent style, with some warmth: neither a default slide nor a scene with facts on top. Plan it with `modules/layout/infographics.md` (INF-1 to INF-6, with LAY-18) before any image.
- **Check:** (a) the hero is the chart, map or diagram, designed in and drawn from the data (INF-1, LAY-19); (b) a confident title on the grid (INF-5); (c) colour codes the structure: one accent, steps or periods each distinct (LAY-18); (d) one reading path with visible connectors; (e) one spot illustration or one icon set, smaller than the information, simplified icons for secondary facts (INF-2); (f) every required line present, letter for letter, and every safety or medical step right (INF-6, CD-7); (g) text on calm ground, big enough for the distance (LAY-15, LAY-7). Then two honest tests. Could it pass for a default slide template? Sterile: strengthen the type, colour structure and hero, not the pictures. Is illustration louder than the information, or are there competing styles? Over-illustrated: cut it back.
- **Fix:** Sterile: a bolder title, a colour code, a bigger hero chart, simplified icons with character, a warm (named) ground. Over-illustrated or chaotic: shrink the spot art, turn pictures into icons, one style, number the path.
- **Why:** Batch 6 (v0.9.4) lost 4 to 6 as sterile, "like a PowerPoint slide" (S63, S65, S66). The v0.9.5 answer, the full pass with drawn scenes, lost batch 7 3 to 7: "way over-illustrated, unbalanced" (S76), charts "pasted on" (S73), a graphic that "doesn't belong" (S74). Both batches' winners were compact and clean, with punchy type, simplified icons and clear flow (S59, S60, S67, S69, S72, S76, S78).
- **Grade:** Procedure. Internal evidence, one judge, two batches; untested until another infographic batch is judged.
