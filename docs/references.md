# Reference library (for calibration)

A short, varied set of public works to calibrate aesthetic judgement (`modules/aesthetic-critique/`). Each entry links to a public page and says, in one or two lines, what works or what goes wrong. **No images are copied here**; open the link. The notes are interpretation by this repo, not the institutions' descriptions.

Use: before judging a piece, pick one or two references in the same purpose or mood and compare *decisions*, not surfaces. Don't imitate a reference; ask what choice made it work and whether a similar choice fits this brief.

All links were checked on 1 November 2026: Met entries through the Met's open collection API (the object page is the canonical `objectURL` it returns), the others by loading the page. Some museum sites block automated fetches; a link that fails for a bot may still work in a browser.

| # | Work | Category | What works (or doesn't) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Katsushika Hokusai, *Under the Wave off Kanagawa* (c. 1830-32), woodblock print. [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45434) | Exceptional composition | A huge foreground curve against a tiny distant mountain: scale contrast and a repeated claw shape drive the eye in a loop. Few colours, clear value structure. |
| 2 | Utagawa Hiroshige, *Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge and Atake* (1857). [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/55433) | Composition; storytelling | Diagonal bridge against slanted lines of rain; tiny hurrying figures make the weather the event. Shows mood carried by line direction alone. |
| 3 | Johannes Vermeer, *The Milkmaid* (c. 1660). [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/SK-A-2344) | Restrained detail | Detail is concentrated on the bread, jug and stream of milk; the wall is nearly empty. Quiet emphasis by where finish is spent. |
| 4 | Josef Albers, *Homage to the Square: With Rays* (1959). [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/489056) | Distinctive colour | Same shape every time; only colour relationships change, and the squares seem to advance or glow. Colour judged relative to its neighbours. |
| 5 | Mark Rothko, *Black on Maroon* (1959). [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/rothko-black-on-maroon-t01163) | Distinctive colour; emotional charge | Close, dark values and soft edges create atmosphere with almost no drawing. Proof that low contrast can be the point. |
| 6 | David Hockney, *A Bigger Splash* (1967). [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/hockney-a-bigger-splash-t03254) | Successful stylisation | Flat, still, geometric setting with one loose burst of paint. The one inconsistency is the subject. |
| 7 | Shigeo Fukuda, *Hiroshima Appeals* (1985), poster. [V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1023301/) | Strong shape language; minimal | One image, one idea, read in a second. A single unexpected shape carries the whole message. |
| 8 | Saul Bass, *Advise & Consent* (1962), film poster. [V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O76008/) | Shape language; cohesive art direction | Cut-paper shapes and hand-made type share one rough edge, so image and words read as one piece. |
| 9 | A. M. Cassandre, *Nord Express* (1927), poster. [V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O89673/) | Successful stylisation; composition | Engine and rails reduced to converging geometry; speed made visible through perspective and repetition. |
| 10 | Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec, *Divan Japonais* (1892-93), lithograph. [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/334197) | Stylisation; cropping | Flat colour, black silhouette and a bold crop that cuts the performer's head off: a rule broken for a reason (the spectator's view). |
| 11 | Vilhelm Hammershøi, *Moonlight, Strandgade 30* (1900-06). [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/441933) | Restrained detail; mood | An almost empty room in a narrow grey range; restraint is the subject. Generic only if you miss the light. |
| 12 | Rembrandt, *The Three Crosses* (1653), drypoint. [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/354631) | Expressive detail; ink | A beam of light and dense, scratched darks; detail where the drama is, rough marks elsewhere. |
| 13 | Käthe Kollwitz, *Widow II*, from *War* (1922-23), woodcut. [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/904964) | Rough but effective | Crude, gouged marks and heavy black. Low finish, high charge: polish would weaken it. |
| 14 | Quentin Blake, illustrator (Roald Dahl books and others). [Official site](https://www.quentinblake.com/) | Rough but effective; ink illustration | Quick, scratchy pen line and loose wash; gesture and character beat accuracy. |
| 15 | Maurice Sendak, *Where the Wild Things Are* (1963). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_the_Wild_Things_Are) | Visual storytelling | Pictures grow across the page as the adventure grows, then shrink back: layout itself tells the story. |
| 16 | Ezra Jack Keats, *The Snowy Day* (1962). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Snowy_Day) | Stylisation; specificity | Collage, patterned paper and simple shapes; a specific child in a specific city, not a generic one. |
| 17 | Jon Klassen, *This Is Not My Hat* (2012). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Not_My_Hat) | Storytelling; restraint | Words and picture disagree, and the joke lives in the gap; muted palette and small eye movements carry the tension. |
| 18 | Shaun Tan, *The Arrival* (2006). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arrival_(book)) | Storytelling; cohesive art direction | Wordless; sepia tone, invented objects and a consistent drawing level make a whole world feel foreign and real. |
| 19 | *Monument Valley* (ustwo games, 2014). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game)) | Shape language; cohesive art direction; 3D | Impossible geometry, soft pastel palette and one tiny figure; every screen obeys the same rules. |
| 20 | *Inside* (Playdead, 2016). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game)) | Cohesive art direction; emotional charge; 3D | Near-monochrome palette with a red-shirted figure; mood held across the whole game by value and colour restraint. |
| 21 | Massimo Vignelli and Unimark, New York subway signage and map (1960s-70s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Vignelli) | Cohesive art direction; information design | One typeface, a strict grid and colour codes; clarity first, and still recognisable decades later. |
| 22 | Milton Glaser, *I ♥ NY* (1977). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Love_New_York) | Minimalist logo | A rebus in a few characters; works at any size and in one colour. |
| 23 | GOV.UK Design System. [GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/) | Restrained UI; coherence | Plain type, high contrast and few components; restraint as the brand. A useful counterweight to "make it pop". |
| 24 | Jamie Reid, *Never Mind the Bollocks* sleeve (1977). [V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1009010/) | Intentional rule-breaking; punk | Clashing fluorescent colours and ransom-note type; the "wrongness" is the message and fits the music. |
| 25 | *Ray Gun* magazine, art direction by David Carson (1990s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Gun_(magazine)) | Intentional rule-breaking | Legibility deliberately traded for mood. Instructive both ways: some spreads work, some are just hard to read. |
| 26 | Corporate Memphis (flat illustration style, late 2010s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_Memphis) | Technically polished but generic | Clean, consistent and on-trend, yet interchangeable between brands: no specificity (AES-6). |
| 27 | Kyle Chayka, "Welcome to AirSpace" (The Verge, 2016). [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2016/8/3/12325104/airbnb-aesthetic-global-minimalism-startup-gentrification) | Polished but generic (critique) | An essay on the look-alike café and app aesthetic: tasteful choices that add up to no place in particular. |
